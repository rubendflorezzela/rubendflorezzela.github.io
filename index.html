I develop human-centered AI systems for safety-critical intelligent transportation, with a focus on driver-state monitoring under real-world conditions.

My research integrates computer vision, gaze dynamics, and multimodal deep learning to detect drowsiness, distraction, and cognitive load — bridging the gap between controlled laboratory models and deployable systems on edge hardware such as the NVIDIA Jetson Nano.

I am particularly interested in understanding how human cognitive states — such as attention, distraction, and workload — interact with AI systems in safety-critical environments. This perspective is grounded in human factors engineering, where models must be accurate, interpretable, auditable, and trustworthy enough to support real-time decisions.

Lecturer and researcher at UNSAAC. Peer reviewer for Scientific Reports, ACM TIST, and 7 other international journals (verified via ORCID).

Unlike purely dataset-driven approaches, my work emphasizes real-time, deployable AI systems validated under real-world driving conditions.
