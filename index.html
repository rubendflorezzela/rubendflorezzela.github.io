I develop interpretable human-centered AI systems for safety-critical intelligent transportation, with a focus on driver-state monitoring under real-world conditions.

My research integrates computer vision, gaze dynamics, and multimodal deep learning to detect drowsiness, distraction, and cognitive load — bridging the gap between controlled laboratory models and deployable systems on edge hardware such as the NVIDIA Jetson Nano. This work is directly relevant to automated driving, advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS), and transportation safety evaluation.

I am particularly interested in how human cognitive states (attention, distraction, and workload) interact with AI systems in high-stakes environments. This perspective is grounded in human factors engineering, where models must be interpretable, trustworthy, and deployable to support real-time decisions.

Lecturer and researcher at UNSAAC. Peer reviewer for Scientific Reports, ACM TIST, and 7 other international journals (verified via ORCID).
