I build human-centered AI systems for driver-state monitoring in intelligent transportation environments. My work sits at the intersection of computer vision, gaze dynamics, and deep learning — focused on detecting drowsiness, distraction, and cognitive load under real-world driving conditions, with deployable inference on edge hardware such as the NVIDIA Jetson Nano.

I am grounded in human factors engineering: the belief that safe AI systems must be interpretable, auditable, and trustworthy enough to inform real decisions in safety-critical environments. My research bridges laboratory-based driver monitoring and practical on-vehicle deployment — addressing the gap most systems never cross.

Lecturer at UNSAAC and peer reviewer for Scientific Reports, ACM TIST, and 7 other international journals (verified via ORCID).
